# dustUP: The Lore of Serket's Reach

The lore bible. Everything the game says about its world (codex pages, Thoth's chatter, boss taunts, loading lines,
the tortoise's gossip) should agree with this page. When the game and this page disagree, fix one of them.

**Tone:** a campfire tall tale told by someone who was almost there. Western myth with Egyptian gods in it. Dry
jokes, big hats, bigger gods. Dark things happen, but we tell them with a squint and a grin, never with gore.
Sundown is cool and heroic, not scary: clean bone, a confident look, a good hat.

---

## The Reach in one breath

Serket's Reach is a desert where the old gods of Egypt never left. They just got hats. There are saloons by the
obelisks, a railroad that runs through the land of the dead, and wanted posters nailed to sphinxes. The gods own the
sand the way ranchers own cattle, and they trade in souls the way ranchers trade in beef.

Then Set, the Red God, rolled his storm across the Reach. A mile-high wall of screaming sand swallowed Serket's
desert, and out of it rode the dead: a hundred waves of them, every outlaw and grudge Set could dig up.

Somebody had to ride into that. The gods hired a dead man.

## Sundown

### The Last Bounty (how he died)

Before he was Sundown he was the best gun between the Flats and the river: a bounty hunter who never missed and
never asked why, and who robbed a tomb or two by moonlight when the bounties ran thin. (The pharaohs never filed a
complaint. The moon did. See Khonsu.)

He rode for ten years with **Marshal Wade Calloway**, a lawman with a fast smile and a tin star. A hunter and a
marshal: the best pair in the Reach. They split every bounty down the middle and every bottle down to the bottom.

The night the storm came, **Sethos**, high priest of Set, made Calloway an offer: a star that never tarnishes and a
life that never ends, in exchange for the one gun in the Reach fast enough to trouble Set's storm. Calloway took it.

He led his partner out onto the hardpan after a bounty that did not exist. The hunter knelt to read the tracks, the
only time anyone ever saw him bow his head, and an **iron bear trap** buried in the sand snapped shut on his jaw.
Calloway tipped his hat, took the hunter's horse, his name and his share, and rode off toward the storm.

The hunter died there at sundown, with iron in his teeth, watching the sun go down over the flats. The wanted posters
needed something to call him. The flats are called **Sundown Flats** now. Every ride begins there.

### The Iron Jaw

The trap had ruined his old skull for good, and Serket spent a whole night trying to pry the iron off what was left.
It had bitten so deep it had become part of the bone. So she did the sensible thing: she kept the teeth, polished
them up and taught them to talk.

The bear trap is a grille of polished iron teeth clamped over his mouthparts now, the scorpion chelicerae Serket gave
him. It snaps shut when he kills, clacks when he reloads, and holds a cigarette better than any lip ever did. He does
not talk much. When he does, it sounds like a gate closing.

### The Remaking

**Serket**, goddess of scorpions, healer of the stung, guardian of the canopic jars and patron saint of hopeless
causes, found two things on the dunes that night:

- the bones of a dead bounty hunter, picked clean and still holding a grudge, and
- the empty carapace of **Aqrab, the Black Stinger**, her champion: a scorpion-man who had held the storm wall for a
  thousand years and fell to Set on the night it broke.

One had a will and no body. The other had a body and no will. Serket stitched them together, and she did not do
it by halves. Nothing of the man's face survived the trap, so nothing of it came back. The remaking was total.

- His head is the Black Stinger's: a skull of bone carapace, a scorpion's head, with the trap's iron teeth set into
  its chelicerae.
- In one of its median eyes she set a drop of her own venom so she could watch the world through it: the green
  **Serket eye**.
- Bone-chitin plates grew over his ribcage like armour grows over a knight.
- He has four arms. His own two, bone and quick, for the gun and the grip; and below them the Black Stinger's great
  skeletal pincers, for everything else. (Thoth thinks this is the best thing that has ever happened. Ozymandias
  notes that he still pays with one hand.)
- The Black Stinger's tail grew out of his spine: segmented bone, a green venom stinger at the tip.
- The hair came back anyway, long and curly, out from under the hat. Serket insists the hair was not her idea. The
  hair disagrees.
- The cigarette and the hat were his idea. Serket says a scorpion in a hat is ridiculous. He says so is a goddess in a
  sandstorm.

Nobody would know him now. That is half the point: the face on his old wanted posters belonged to a man who died.

He woke up on Sundown Flats with a new wanted poster flapping on the signpost, a scorpion skull under a hat where the
portrait should be: DEAD OR ALIVE, SUNDOWN, NAME UNKNOWN, $1,000,000. Set put that price on his skull, because a
dead man Set cannot keep is a problem. There is also a million on Sethos. Ozymandias says that makes them even.
Sundown says it makes them a matched pair.

### The Debt at the Scales (the soul jar)

When he died, his heart went to **Anubis** and the Scales of Ma'at in the Hall of Two Truths. A bounty hunter and a
tomb robber: it was heavy. Ammit, the Devourer, was already licking her lips.

Serket stepped in and asked for the heart **on credit**. Anubis, who is a fair god and a careful bookkeeper, agreed
to a loan: the hunter goes back up and sends ahead the souls that have cheated the scales, every husk, mummy and
outlaw Set raised out of the storm. Each one he puts down is a payment.

The souls he reaps pour into **Serket's canopic jar**, the glass jar in the HUD with Pinch standing guard on the lid.
Below it beats his own heart, wrapped in linen and stitched back into his ribs. When the jar is full he can uncork it
and spend the souls on **Serket's Wrath**. Spending souls is borrowing against the debt, which is why the debt never
seems to get any smaller. Anubis wrote the total on a papyrus. Thoth read it and had to sit down.

When he falls in the Reach, Anubis drags him down and weighs him again. A light heart walks back up into the sun:
"Go back. Finish your hunt."

### Why he wakes up at home

Fall a second time and Anubis takes the soul. But a soul that is still owed is **collateral**, and collateral goes back
in the jar. The jar goes home to the hideout, where **Mama Toad** sets it on the mantel. Then the bones need nights to
knit, and nights cost something. Thoth pays for them out of the five days he once won from the moon (see Khonsu). Some
rides that takes a day. Some rides it takes a week. Then Sundown wakes up on the couch, sore, hungry, and in no mood
to discuss it. He has never asked how it works. He prefers not to.

### Why he hunts bounties for the gods

At the dawn of the world Thoth wrote the **Treaty of the Sands**: no god may raise a hand in another god's desert. It
kept the peace for ten thousand years. Then Set found the loophole: a storm is not a hand.

The other gods cannot ride into Set's storm without breaking the Treaty and starting a war. A dead man is not a god.
So they hire him. The gods post the bounties, and they pay twice:

- in **gold** from their tombs (most of it ends up in Ozymandias' till), and
- in **letters**. Every bounty he brings down, Serket carves one letter of his real name back onto his soul. She has
  carved a few so far. None of them are vowels.

Serket wants her sands back. Sundown wants his name back. Funny how the stars line up.

## Pinch

Pinch is **the last of Serket's brood**. Set stole the rest of her scorpions in the storm and twisted them into the
giant scorpions that hunt the dunes now. Pinch refuses to look at them.

When Aqrab fell, he was carrying one last egg in the joint of his shell. Serket did not notice it until the remaking
was done and the egg cracked open in an empty socket of the hunter's new skull. Pinch hatched first and saw Sundown
second. Scorpions imprint. Pinch has been trying to raise him ever since. ("Pinch, you have raised him well," Serket
told him. Pinch took it as a compliment. Sundown did not.)

Because they came out of the same shell, they are bound: Pinch can never wander far, and Sundown can never quite get
rid of him. Pinch lives in the hat by day and curls up in that warm empty socket at night, right beside the Serket
eye, which is why it glows brighter when he sleeps. He talks in clicks. Serket translates when she feels like it, and
he is mostly saying that Sundown is rusty, reckless and snores. He adores him. He milks his own venom for the
bullets. Do not ask how.

He insists the phantom scorpions that sometimes hatch from Sundown's kills are his cousins. He insists Big Bertha is
no relation at all.

## The gods

- **Serket**, the Scorpion Goddess. Heals the poisoned and poisons the proud. Guardian of the canopic jars. Kind,
  sharp and always three moves ahead. She appears out of the sand as a girl of jade light with a scorpion's tail.
- **Anubis**, Weigher of Hearts. Not cruel, just exact. Keeps the scales, the ledgers and the Gate of the West. His
  jackal temple guard took Set's gold, which embarrasses him deeply.
- **Thoth**, Scribe of the Gods. Ten thousand and three years old (and a half), about knee high, ibis-headed, and he
  talks like a delighted child. He invented writing, then doodling, and he thinks doodling is better. He wrote the
  Treaty of the Sands. He writes down every soul sent ahead.
- **Khonsu**, the Moon Who Wanders. God of the moon and of time. Long ago he played senet with Thoth for a share of
  his moonlight, lost five whole days, and has been grumpy about it ever since. Khonsu remembers every grave robbed
  by moonlight and every night ever borrowed, and Sundown has done plenty of both.
- **Set**, the Red God. Lord of the desert and the storm and of everything that does not want you here. He got the
  worst sand when the gods split the world, and the blame for everything else. Now he wants all of it.
- **Ra**, the Sun King. The oldest light. Tired of storms, tired of nights, tired of the Reach. His answer to every
  problem is to burn it.
- **Khepri**, who rolls the sun up every morning. One of his sons ran off with a ball of his own (the Sunroller).
- **Sekhmet** sleeps in the linen of the cursed mummies, and you do not want her to wake up.

## Thoth's Hall and Sundown's Ledger

Thoth's house of records sinks slowly into the Bonewind Dunes. Inside it is enormous: a nave of sandstone and gold
under a lapis vault, bookcases to the ceiling, flying books, and **bookworm librarians**, real eyeless earthworms who
read by touch, file everything under S for shelf and have opinions about vellum.

Thoth's problem is Sundown. Every soul sent ahead gets one line in Thoth's big book, and Sundown has been written
down as dead far too many times. It ruins the records. So Thoth did what any scribe would do: he started a
collection. Sundown is his favourite specimen, and the hunter's whole story goes into **Sundown's Ledger**, page by
page, as it comes out. Every creature, boss and trinket the hunter finds gets a jar in the Hall. Every study the
hunter finishes earns a present from Thoth's sock drawer.

Thoth knows Sundown's real name. He wrote it down the night he died. He is not allowed to say it, because a name given
back has to be earned. He nearly says it about once a visit.

## Mark and the moon

At the Heart of the Oasis, where Khonsu sleeps under the sand, there was a temple, and the temple had a guardian:
**Mark**, a black-furred cat warrior wrapped in moon-blessed linen, sworn to silence so that only the moon would speak
for him. He kept that vow for three thousand years while the oasis flooded over the temple.

When **Khonsu of the Sands** falls, the moon god's grip on the oasis breaks, and so does the oath. Mark is free. He
rides with Sundown now on a crescent board, fighting with twin batons and moon crests. Why? Cats choose their people.
Also Khonsu asked him to keep an eye on the man who keeps borrowing nights. Both things are true. Mark says nothing
about either.

His temple name is forty syllables long and only the moon may say it. Sundown calls him Mark, after the crescent mark
on his brow. Mark allows it.

## The river: Cleopatra, the Sphinx and the Burning

Beyond the oasis, once the moon lets go, the river road opens: **the Nile**, Ra's river.

- **Cleopatra**, Avatar of Ra and Daughter of the Sun, rules the river from a golden barge. She is veiled head to
  foot in ivory silk because her face is made of sunlight, and anyone who sees it burns. Her father poured himself
  into her so he could walk the world without setting it on fire. She commands a river army of jackal spearmen,
  falcons, baboons, rams, war hippos and barges, led by **Sebekhotep**, the Jaw of the Nile, a crocodile she has fed
  her enemies to for a thousand floods, and **the Golden Leviathan**, flagship of the Queen's river.
- **The Sphinx**, Keeper of the River Gate, lies before the bronze gate at wave 50. It asks every traveller a riddle
  and plays with them like a cat with a toy. It has not lost since the pyramids were pointy.
- **Ra** has decided the only cure for Set's storm and Khonsu's long nights is **the Burning**: a noon that never
  ends, the sun nailed to the top of the sky until the whole Reach is clean, bright glass. Cleopatra rides ahead to
  make the river ready. If she falls, her father comes himself, and the river burns.

Ozymandias keeps saying "it is always noon here." He means it as a joke. It is less of a joke every year.

## The bosses in the story

- **The Avatar of Khonsu** (waves 5, 35, 65): the moon's collector, sent to take back the nights Sundown borrowed.
- **Khonsu of the Sands** (wave 75): the god himself, standing up out of the oasis in the shape of the desert.
  His fall frees Mark and opens the river.
- **The Dust Marshal** (waves 10, 25, 40): Wade Calloway. Set kept his promise the way a storm keeps a house. Calloway
  died anyway, was buried with his star, and Set dug him back up as the last law of the dunes. He still calls Sundown
  "partner". He escapes twice. The third time he means to finish what he started.
- **Sethos** (waves 50, 85): high priest of Set, Hand of Set, Voice of the Storm, the man who bought the trap.
- **The Avatar of Set** (wave 100): at the Apex of the Red Pyramid the priest burns away and the god walks in his skin.
  Nobody has written that page yet.
- **The Ironclad**: the fireman on the Set Express, the railroad Set laid through the Duat. Now he is the express.
- **Pharaoh Nekh-Amun**: a king the storm woke, who refuses to lie back down.
- **Big Bertha, Old Gulp, Old Thornback, the Sunroller, the Oasis King, the Olm**: the wild powers of the Reach. They
  serve nobody, but the storm made all of them meaner.

## Friends of the Reach

- **Mama Toad** found the hideout before Sundown did and let him stay. She knits, cooks, worries, keeps the soul jar on
  the mantel and never asks where the bodies came from.
- **Ozymandias** keeps the only shop that walks, on the back of **Gobi**, a giant wandering turtle with a tree on its
  shell. Nine hundred years on the road. He sold a young bounty hunter his first shovel, and he sold a marshal a bear
  trap, and he did not ask what for. He wishes he had asked. Thoth says Ozymandias was a king once, and that he told Ra
  his statues were taller than the sun. Ozymandias says Thoth says a lot of things.
- **Mother Tamsin**, a two-hundred-year-old tortoise haggler. Everything she sells is cursed only a little.
- **Cactus Pete**, an old gold panner with a broken wagon. **Doc Hapi**, a snake-oil peddler who once left a crate of
  tonic at Thoth's Hall and never came back for it.
- **The meerkats** trade anything for shiny things, and four of them, the Beatle Meerkats, only play when the colony is
  safe and only know the old songs.

## Timeline

1. **Before the sands.** The gods divide the world into deserts. Thoth writes the Treaty of the Sands.
2. **The Old Kingdom.** Thoth wins five days from Khonsu at senet. Khepri starts rolling the sun; one of his sons
   rolls off with his own. Serket's champion Aqrab takes up his post at the edge of her desert.
3. **About 3,000 years ago.** Mark takes his vow of silence in the moon temple at the Heart of the Oasis.
4. **About 900 years ago.** Ozymandias starts walking the road on Gobi's back.
5. **The Gold Years** (the last century). Railroads, saloons and wanted posters come to the Reach. A young bounty
   hunter buys his first shovel from a tortoise. He and Marshal Wade Calloway ride together for ten years.
6. **The Night of the Storm** (year zero). Set's storm wall rolls over Serket's Reach. Aqrab falls at the wall.
   Sethos buys Calloway. The bear trap closes on Sundown Flats at sundown.
7. **The First Weighing.** The heart is heavy. Serket takes it on credit.
8. **The Remaking.** Bone and carapace, a scorpion skull, four arms, the iron teeth, the Serket eye. Pinch hatches.
9. **Soon after.** Calloway dies anyway and is buried with his star. Set raises him as the Dust Marshal. Set lays the
   railroad through the Duat and puts the Ironclad on it.
10. **The Ride** (the game). Sundown wakes on Sundown Flats under his own wanted poster. Serket rises out of the sand
    and points him at the storm.
11. **The Oasis.** Khonsu of the Sands falls. Mark is free. The river road opens.
12. **The River.** The Sphinx at the gate, Cleopatra on her barge, and Ra behind her with a sun in each hand.
13. **The Apex.** The Avatar of Set waits at the top of the Red Pyramid. The last page of the Ledger is blank.

## Where the lore lives in the game

- `src/game/meta/Lore.ts`: Sundown's Ledger pages (unlocked through play, shown in the Legends wing of Thoth's Hall),
  loading-screen lore lines (`ui/LoadingScreen.ts` alternates them with the tips), death-screen lines
  (`scenes/DeathCine.ts`, under "N DAYS PASSED").
- `src/game/meta/Codex.ts`: codex entries (Legends hold the Ledger pages) and the page triggers (`loreCheck`,
  `loreBoss`, `hallVisit`); `src/game/meta/Research.ts`: their studies.
