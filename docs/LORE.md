# dustUP: The Lore of Serket's Reach

The lore bible. Everything the game says about its world (codex pages, Thoth's chatter, boss taunts, loading lines,
the tortoise's gossip, the cinematics) should agree with this page. When the game and this page disagree, fix one of
them.

**Theme:** the tarot and the gods and stories of old Egypt. Every card is a page of the Book of Thoth; every god keeps
a trump; every boss, quest and stranger is an old story with sand in its boots.

**Tone:** a campfire tall tale told by someone who was almost there. Western myth with Egyptian gods in it. Dry
jokes, big hats, bigger gods. Dark things happen, but we tell them with a squint and a grin, never with gore.
Sundown is cool and heroic, not scary: clean polished bone, a confident look, a good hat.

**Humans are shadows.** Every human in the Reach (travellers, outlaws, townsfolk, his own mother and father) is a
completely black, featureless figure, like a shadow on a tent wall. Gods, beasts and the cursed have faces. Humans do
not, because to the gods they all look alike.

---

## The Reach in one breath

Serket's Reach is a desert where the old gods of Egypt never left. They just got hats. There are saloons by the
obelisks, a railroad that runs through the land of the dead, card tables in every tomb and wanted posters nailed to
sphinxes. The gods own the sand the way ranchers own cattle, and they trade in souls the way ranchers trade in beef.

Then Set, the Red God, rolled his storm across the Reach. A mile-high wall of screaming sand swallowed Serket's
desert, and out of it rode the dead: wave after wave, day after day, every outlaw and grudge Set could dig up. The
nights are worse.

Somebody had to ride into that. The gods hired a cursed child who grew up.

## The Book of Thoth (the tarot)

The oldest story in the Reach says the tarot was never a game. Thoth wrote it: twenty-two trumps that hold the whole
world in pictures, so that the gods could read a fate without saying it out loud. Card readers deal from copies of it
in every caravan and saloon. The real book lives in Thoth's Hall, and some of its pages have gone missing. The cards
Sundown fights with are those pages (the tarot pile in the HUD).

Each god keeps a trump. The ones the story leans on:

| Trump | Holder | What it means in the Reach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 The Fool | Sundown | the wanderer with no number, as Sundown has no name |
| I The Magician | Thoth | the scribe who wrote the deck and cheats at senet |
| II The High Priestess | Isis | the mother of magic, who once hid in the marshes |
| VIII Justice | Ma'at and Anubis | the feather, the scales and the heart |
| XI Strength | Sekhmet | the lioness asleep in the mummies' linen |
| IX The Hermit | Ozymandias | the old wanderer with a lantern and a shop on his back |
| XII The Hanged Man | Mark | the vow of silence, the patience of the moon |
| X The Wheel of Fortune | Khepri | the wheel of the sun, rolled up every morning |
| XIII Death | Anubis | not an ending: a weighing |
| XVI The Tower | Set | the storm, the fall, the ruin of every house |

| XVIII The Moon | Khonsu | the long night, every lie told in the dark |
| XIX The Sun | Ra | the noon that never ends |

## Sundown

### The Seven Scorpions (why he was born cursed)

This is a real old story. When Isis fled with the baby Horus, Serket sent seven scorpions to guard them. One night
Isis begged shelter from a rich woman, who shut her door in the goddess's face. The smallest scorpion, Tefen, crept
under that door and stung the rich woman's son. Isis healed the boy, because she was kind. But a door shut on a
mother is a debt, and the sand remembers debts. Every so many generations a child of that house is born with the sting
still in him.

Sundown was that child. He came into the world in a travellers' camp in the deep sand with a little tail, two tiny
claws and a soft amber carapace. His mother sang to him anyway.

### The Camp at Dawn (how he was left)

His parents were travellers: a caravan of card readers and camel traders who followed the wells. The night he was
born, his father dealt the Book of Thoth over the cradle and turned up **The Tower**, **The Moon** and **Death**: ruin,
a lie in the dark, an ending. He read it as a curse on the whole caravan. He could not look at the boy.

His mother held him by the fire and wept until her shoulders shook. She whispered a name into his ear, the only time
anyone ever said it, and before dawn she slipped one card into his swaddle: **The Star**.

At dawn the camp was gone. Tents struck, camels loaded, tracks going up over the dune into the sunrise. His mother
looked back once. The baby lay by the cold fire with a blue thread tied round his swaddle and a card against his
chest. The sand kept him. Something small came up out of it and sat beside him until the heat of the day. (Nobody
has said what it was. See Pinch.)

He still carries The Star. It is the first card of every deck he deals. His own card is **The Fool**: the trump with
no number, for the boy with no name.

### The Scavenger Years (the iron jaw)

He grew up wild in the dunes: jackals would not bite him and scorpions would not sting him, because they knew their
own. He ate what the rail camps threw out and slept in robbed tombs. Behind one kitchen a big kind frog left a plate of
scraps on the step every night and never asked his name (Mama Toad remembers; ask her).

When he was about ten he crept into a trapper's cache on the flats at sundown, and an **iron bear trap** snapped shut
on his face. The trapper was a young lawman called **Wade Calloway**. He found the scorpion-boy in his trap, laughed,
and rode away. It was the second time Sundown was left.

He got out at last with the trap still clamped on his mouthparts. It never came off. It grew into him, the way iron
grows into a tree. He filed the teeth, polished the springs and learned to talk through it. Now the bear trap is a
grille of polished iron teeth over his chelicerae: it snaps shut when he kills, clacks when he reloads, and holds a
cigarette better than any lip ever did. When he talks, it sounds like a gate closing. The flats where it happened are
called **Sundown Flats** now, and so is he.

### The Bounty Hunter

Nobody hires a scorpion-man for honest work. Bounties do not ask. The only reading he ever learned was wanted
posters. The only riding he ever learned was a scrap of driftwood on a dune face, which became the fastest board in
the Reach. By twenty he was the best gun between the Flats and the river: four arms, a tail, two pistols' worth of
patience, and he always came at sundown, with the sun in his targets' eyes.

What he looks like now: a lean scorpion-man about 1.6 m tall, his carapace bleached pale as bone over a ribcage you
can see through, so people call him a revenant (he keeps coming back). A full scorpion head with modelled median eyes;
the iron bear-trap grille; long curly hard-rock hair out from under a battered hat; a cigarette. Two bony humanoid arms
for the gun and the grip; below them a pair of big pincer arms for everything else. A segmented bone tail with a green
venom stinger. No glasses. Cool, confident eyes.

### The First Job (the tomb in the Weeping Wall)

His first bounty from the gods had no name on it, only a mark: a scorpion pressed in green wax, and a reward big
enough to buy a town. It led him to the **Weeping Wall**, a great cliff of red stone at the edge of the Reach, and to
a door cut into it: the tomb of **Aqrab, the Black Stinger**, Serket's champion, who held the storm wall for a
thousand years and fell to Set the night it broke.

Inside, in the dust on the sarcophagus, sat a little scorpion. It looked at him. The doors slammed shut behind him.
Then the little scorpion stood up out of itself, and up, and up, and was the goddess **Serket**.

She had posted the bounty. She had been waiting for him since that dawn by the cold fire. "A child of my sting," she
said, "grown up. Good. I need a new champion, and you are going to earn it." Then she opened her **domain** and shut
him inside it.

### Serket's Domain (the trial)

Serket's domain is a training arena between the sand and the stars, all jade light and old bones. She trapped him
there and taught him, piece by piece: the board, the jumps and the long jump, the flips, the cards, the heart and the
gun. Then she summoned a **soul scorpion** out of the floor and told him to kill it. When he did, she finished what the
sting had begun: she woke the venom in his tail, set a drop of her own sight in one of his median eyes (the green
**Serket eye**, so she can watch the world through it), and bound herself to him in her smallest shape.

### Why he hunts bounties for the gods

At the dawn of the world Thoth wrote the **Treaty of the Sands**: no god may raise a hand in another god's desert. It
kept the peace for ten thousand years. Then Set found the loophole: a storm is not a hand.

The other gods cannot ride into Set's storm without breaking the Treaty. A cursed child is not a god, and not quite a
man either. So they hire him. The gods post the bounties, and they pay twice:

- in **gold** from their tombs (most of it ends up in Ozymandias' till), and
- in **his name**. His mother whispered it once; Thoth heard it, and wrote it down. Every bounty he brings in, Serket
  carves one letter of it back onto his heart. She has carved a few so far. None of them are vowels.

Serket wants her sands back. Sundown wants his name. Funny how the stars line up.

### The Weighing (why he wakes up at home)

When he falls in the Reach, **Anubis** drags him down to the Hall of Two Truths and sets his heart on the Scales of
Ma'at against the feather. Serket stands surety for him, so the first falls are forgiven: drag the heart onto the
scale, balance it, walk back up into the sun. "Go back. Finish your hunt."

But every fall leaves something on the heart, and it gets heavier. After the third, it is too heavy to balance. Then
Anubis sends him home to heal: to the hideout, where Mama Toad keeps the kettle on. The bones need nights to knit, and
nights cost something. Thoth pays for them out of the five days he once won from the moon (see Khonsu). Some rides
that takes a day. Some rides it takes a week. Then Sundown wakes up on the couch, sore, hungry, and in no mood to
discuss it.

## Pinch

Pinch is **Serket herself**, made small. The little scorpion from the tomb never left: she rides in his hat by day and
curls up beside the Serket eye at night, which is why it glows brighter when she sleeps. In that shape she can cross
the Treaty without breaking it (a scorpion is not a hand either). She talks in clicks, and she whispers to him through
them: advice, warnings, opinions about his hat. Mostly she says he is reckless, rusty and snores.

Why the name: he called the little scorpion "Pinch" the first night, before he knew. She has never corrected him. When
the ultimate comes she grows back into her true size, a scorpion big enough to stand on a dune, and everything nearby
learns why.

Set stole the rest of her brood in the storm and twisted them into the giant scorpions that hunt the dunes. Pinch will
not look at them. She insists the phantom scorpions that hatch from Sundown's kills are her children's ghosts. She
insists Big Bertha is no relation at all.

And yes: the something small that sat with the baby by the cold fire was her. She will not say so. She clicks and
changes the subject.

## The gods

- **Serket**, the Scorpion Goddess. The Star. Heals the stung and stings the proud; protector of mothers and of the
  canopic jars. Kind, sharp and always three moves ahead. In the world she appears as a girl of jade light with a
  scorpion's tail, or as the little scorpion on his hat.
- **Isis**, the High Priestess, mother of magic. The seven scorpions guarded her. She healed the boy her scorpion
  stung, and she has never quite forgiven Serket for the rest of it.
- **Anubis**, Weigher of Hearts. Death and Justice both. Not cruel, just exact. Keeps the scales, the ledgers and the
  Gate of the West. His jackal temple guard took Set's gold, which embarrasses him deeply.
- **Thoth**, the Magician, Scribe of the Gods. Ten thousand and three years old (and a half), about knee high,
  ibis-headed, and he talks like a delighted child. He invented writing, then doodling, then the tarot, and he thinks
  doodling is best. He wrote the Treaty of the Sands. He writes down every soul sent ahead. He keeps his distance until
  Serket's trial is done (her champion first, his collection second).
- **Khonsu**, the Moon Who Wanders. God of the moon and of time. Long ago he played senet with Thoth for a share of his
  moonlight, lost five whole days, and has been grumpy about it ever since. He remembers every grave robbed by
  moonlight and every night borrowed.
- **Set**, the Red God, the Tower. Lord of the desert and the storm and of everything that does not want you here. He
  got the worst sand when the gods split the world, and the blame for everything else. Now he wants all of it.
- **Ra**, the Sun King. The oldest light. Tired of storms, tired of nights, tired of the Reach. His answer to every
  problem is to burn it.
- **Khepri**, who rolls the sun up every morning. One of his sons ran off with a ball of his own (the Sunroller).
- **Sekhmet** sleeps in the linen of the cursed mummies, and you do not want her to wake up.

## Thoth's Hall and Sundown's Ledger

Thoth's house of records sinks slowly into the Bonewind Dunes. Inside it is enormous: a nave of sandstone and gold
under a lapis vault, bookcases to the ceiling, flying books, the Book of Thoth on a lectern with pages missing, and
**bookworm librarians**, real eyeless earthworms who read by touch, file everything under S for shelf and have
opinions about vellum.

Thoth collects. Every soul sent ahead gets one line in his big book, but a cursed child who keeps coming back ruins the
records, so Sundown became his favourite specimen, and the hunter's whole story goes into **Sundown's Ledger**, page
by page, as it comes out. Every creature, boss and trinket gets a jar in the Hall once it has been hunted down (a
creature is only discovered once it is killed). Every study the hunter finishes earns a present from Thoth's sock
drawer.

Thoth knows Sundown's real name. He heard the mother whisper it and wrote it down the night the baby was born. He is
not allowed to say it, because a name given back has to be earned. He nearly says it about once a visit.

## Mark and the moon

At the Heart of the Oasis, where Khonsu sleeps under the sand, there was a temple, and the temple had a guardian:
**Mark**, a black-furred cat warrior wrapped in moon-blessed linen, sworn to silence so that only the moon would speak
for him. He kept that vow for three thousand years while the oasis flooded over the temple.

When **Khonsu of the Sands** falls, the moon god's grip on the oasis breaks, and so does the oath. Mark is free. He
rides with Sundown now on a crescent board, fighting with twin batons and moon crests. Why? Cats choose their people.
Also Khonsu asked him to keep an eye on the man who keeps borrowing nights. Both things are true. Mark says nothing
about either.

His temple name is forty syllables long and only the moon may say it. Sundown calls him Mark, after the crescent mark
on his brow. Mark allows it.

## The river: Cleopatra, the Sphinx and the Burning

Beyond the oasis, once the moon lets go, the river road opens: **the Nile**, Ra's river.

- **Cleopatra**, Avatar of Ra and Daughter of the Sun, rules the river from a golden barge. She is veiled head to
  foot in ivory silk because her face is made of sunlight, and anyone who sees it burns. Her father poured himself
  into her so he could walk the world without setting it on fire. She commands a river army of jackal spearmen,
  falcons, baboons, rams, war hippos and barges, led by **Sebekhotep**, the Jaw of the Nile, a crocodile she has fed
  her enemies to for a thousand floods, and **the Golden Leviathan**, flagship of the Queen's river.
- **The Sphinx**, Keeper of the River Gate, lies before the bronze gate at wave 50. It asks every traveller a riddle
  and plays with them like a cat with a toy. It has not lost since the pyramids were pointy.
- **Ra** has decided the only cure for Set's storm and Khonsu's long nights is **the Burning**: a noon that never
  ends, the sun nailed to the top of the sky until the whole Reach is clean, bright glass. Cleopatra rides ahead to
  make the river ready. If she falls, her father comes himself, and the river burns.

Ozymandias keeps saying "it is always noon here." He means it as a joke. It is less of a joke every year.

## The bosses in the story

- **The Avatar of Khonsu** (waves 5, 35, 65): the moon's collector, sent to take back the nights Sundown borrowed.
- **Khonsu of the Sands** (wave 75): the god himself, standing up out of the oasis in the shape of the desert.
  His fall frees Mark and opens the river.
- **The Dust Marshal** (waves 10, 25, 40): Wade Calloway, the lawman whose trap is Sundown's grille. Sethos offered him a
  star that never tarnishes and a life that never ends, for the head of the scorpion-boy who got away. Calloway took it,
  died anyway, was buried with his star, and Set dug him back up as the last law of the dunes. Set keeps a promise the
  way a storm keeps a house. He still calls Sundown "kid". He escapes twice. The third time he means to finish what he
  started.
- **Sethos** (waves 50, 85): high priest of Set, Hand of Set, Voice of the Storm, the man who bought Calloway.
- **The Avatar of Set** (wave 100): at the Apex of the Red Pyramid the priest burns away and the god walks in his skin.
  Nobody has written that page yet.
- **The Ironclad**: the fireman on the Set Express, the railroad Set laid through the Duat. Now he is the express.
- **Pharaoh Nekh-Amun**: a king the storm woke, who refuses to lie back down.
- **Big Bertha, Old Gulp, Old Thornback, the Sunroller, the Oasis King, the Olm**: the wild powers of the Reach. They
  serve nobody, but the storm made all of them meaner.

## Friends of the Reach

- **Mama Toad** left scraps on her kitchen step for a scorpion-boy years ago, and she remembers him (he pretends he does
  not remember her). She found the hideout before he did and let him stay. She knits, cooks, worries, keeps the kettle
  on for the nights he comes home broken, and never asks where the bodies came from.
- **Ozymandias** keeps the only shop that walks, on the back of **Gobi**, a giant wandering turtle with a tree on its
  shell. Nine hundred years on the road. He sold a scavenger boy his first shovel, and he sold a young lawman a bear
  trap, and he did not ask what for. He wishes he had asked. Thoth says Ozymandias was a king once, and that he told Ra
  his statues were taller than the sun. Ozymandias says Thoth says a lot of things.
- **Mother Tamsin**, a two-hundred-year-old tortoise haggler. Everything she sells is cursed only a little.
- **Cactus Pete**, an old gold panner with a broken wagon. **Doc Hapi**, a snake-oil peddler who once left a crate of
  tonic at Thoth's Hall and never came back for it.
- **The meerkats** trade anything for shiny things, and four of them, the Beatle Meerkats, only play when the colony is
  safe and only know the old songs.

Gods and storytellers wait by the trail with a job (`src/game/run/MythRoad.ts`). Shots never land on them: the round
sparks off and they tell you what they think of that. The job's title is a song.

**Mojo, the Fool's dog.** Every tarot Fool has a little dog at his heels. Sundown's is a scruffy wire-haired terrier
who loves coffee gum more than life (the turtle shop sells it; Bes gives it away). No gum, and he snatches a fistful
of gold and bolts. Run him down while he pants, or bribe him. Fed, he digs up gold he buried earlier.

**Mama Toad on the road.** Some rides she is out in the dunes by a puddle, crying for her five toadlets. It is the
same toad from the hideout, and she knows him: the scraps on her kitchen step, the boy hiding under it when the
lawman rode by, the nights on her couch (`meta/ToadMemory`).

## Timeline

1. **Before the sands.** The gods divide the world into deserts. Thoth writes the Treaty of the Sands, and the Book of
   Thoth (the tarot).
2. **The Old Kingdom.** Thoth wins five days from Khonsu at senet. Khepri starts rolling the sun. Isis flees with
   Horus; Serket's seven scorpions guard them; a rich woman shuts her door, and Tefen stings her son. Serket's champion
   Aqrab takes up his post at the edge of her desert.
3. **About 3,000 years ago.** Mark takes his vow of silence in the moon temple at the Heart of the Oasis.
4. **About 900 years ago.** Ozymandias starts walking the road on Gobi's back.
5. **About thirty years ago.** A cursed child is born in a travellers' camp. His father draws the Tower, the Moon and
   Death. His mother hides The Star in his swaddle. At dawn the camp is gone.
6. **The scavenger years.** Scraps on Mama Toad's step. Calloway's bear trap on the flats at sundown.
7. **The bounty years.** The boy with the iron jaw becomes Sundown, the best gun in the Reach.
8. **The Night of the Storm.** Set's storm wall rolls over Serket's Reach. Aqrab falls at the wall. Sethos buys
   Calloway; Calloway dies anyway and rises as the Dust Marshal.
9. **The First Job** (the game begins). A nameless bounty leads Sundown to Aqrab's tomb in the Weeping Wall. The little
   scorpion, the doors, the goddess. Serket's domain and the soul scorpion. Pinch rides with him.
10. **The Days.** Out into the storm, a day at a time, wave after wave; the nights are harder.
11. **The Oasis.** Khonsu of the Sands falls. Mark is free. The river road opens.
12. **The River.** The Sphinx at the gate, Cleopatra on her barge, and Ra behind her with a sun in each hand.
13. **The Apex.** The Avatar of Set waits at the top of the Red Pyramid. The last page of the Ledger is blank.

## Where the lore lives in the game

- `src/game/cine/Backstory.ts`: the backstory cinematic (the camp, the dawn, the grown hunter). The mother, the father
  and every other human are shadows (`src/game/models/shadowHuman.ts`).
- `src/game/ui/loading/MemoryShards.ts`: the first-boot loading screen, "collecting pieces of memory" (its seven lines
  are fragments of the backstory).
- `src/game/meta/Lore.ts`: Sundown's Ledger pages (unlocked through play, shown in the Legends wing of Thoth's Hall),
  loading-screen lore lines (`ui/LoadingScreen.ts` alternates them with the tips), death-screen lines
  (`scenes/DeathCine.ts`, under "N DAYS PASSED").
- `src/game/meta/Codex.ts`: codex entries (Legends hold the Ledger pages) and the page triggers (`loreCheck`,
  `loreBoss`, `hallVisit`); `src/game/meta/Research.ts`: their studies.
- `src/game/run/NewGame.ts`, `src/game/cine/TombIntro.ts`, `src/game/run/SerketDomain.ts`: the new game (memory pieces,
  backstory, the lore title, the tomb in the Weeping Wall, Serket's domain).
- `src/game/scenes/thoth/ThothTalk.ts`: Thoth's lines about every page, his lore greetings and idle gossip.
- `src/game/audio/Barks.ts`: Sundown's boss-entrance quips and Pinch's reactions (floating 3D text).
- `src/game/ui/Shop.ts`: Ozymandias' memories. `src/game/scenes/HubScene.ts`: Mama Toad.
